Introducing Acme.ai

August 29, 2024
Acme teamProduct notes · Design preview

A useful tool should make the next step easier to see. That is the idea behind Acme.ai: a shared place for the information, questions, and decisions that move a team forward.

We designed this product concept around a familiar working day. A brief arrives in one place, the supporting data lives somewhere else, and the person making the decision needs a clear picture of both. Bringing that picture together should feel straightforward.

Start with the work, not the dashboard

Most teams already have plenty of information. The difficult part is knowing what is relevant, how it connects, and what to do with it. A new dashboard only helps if it makes those questions easier to answer.

Acme.ai begins with a focused workflow. Choose the references that belong to the task, give the analysis a clear purpose, and keep the result connected to its context. The goal is a useful handoff from information to action.

  • Keep project context in an understandable working space.
  • Make the question and the intended outcome explicit.
  • Give every result a clear path to review and discussion.

A few connected capabilities

The concept brings four familiar activities together. The dashboard offers an overview; a conversational interface helps someone ask a better question; trend analysis provides another perspective; and reporting turns the useful findings into something a colleague can review.

Each capability should support the others. A chart without its question is hard to judge. A recommendation without its evidence is hard to trust. A report without a next step is easy to forget.

Capability What it contributes
Shared dashboard A consistent view of the current work
Natural-language questions A familiar way to explore the context
Trend analysis A starting point for further investigation
Reporting A considered summary for the next conversation

Keep people in the loop

Automation is useful when its boundaries are clear. The person reviewing an outcome needs to understand what information was considered, where uncertainty remains, and which decisions still belong to them.

A good workflow leaves the next person with less to reconstruct and more room to think.

That is why review is a separate step in the Acme concept. It creates a moment to inspect the result, ask a follow-up question, and choose whether the proposed next action makes sense.

Begin with one real question

A small first project is often the most informative. Pick a recurring piece of work with a clear owner and a recognizable finish. Bring only the context it needs. Notice where the process becomes easier and where it needs more explanation.

  1. Choose a question that your team already understands.
  2. Collect the relevant references and define a useful output.
  3. Review the result together before expanding the workflow.

This website is a static demonstration of that experience. Its dashboard images, plan options, and forms illustrate a product direction. They are not connected to an AI service or account system.
