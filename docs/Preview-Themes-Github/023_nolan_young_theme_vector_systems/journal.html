Product notes / 01

Why environment policies belong in the workflow

Teams often describe their release process in one place and perform it in another. That separation makes it easy for expectations to drift. An environment policy offers a shared description of what a change needs before it moves forward.

The useful unit is a decision: who reviews a production release, which checks matter, and where an exception is recorded. A policy that makes these decisions visible gives the team something concrete to improve.

Engineering practice / 02

Service ownership is a relationship

A name in a catalog is a starting point. Useful ownership also explains what a team maintains, where a service fits, and how to ask for help. The description should help someone outside the team make progress.

Review ownership when responsibilities change, not just when a new service is created. A short, accurate record is more useful than a comprehensive catalog nobody trusts.

Delivery / 03

Make a release easy to explain

A good release record answers three questions: what changed, why it changed, and what happened afterward. Connecting source, review, environment, and outcome turns a collection of events into a coherent story.

That story matters during an incident, but it also matters in ordinary work. Teams can learn from a release when the context is still available after the conversation has moved on.
