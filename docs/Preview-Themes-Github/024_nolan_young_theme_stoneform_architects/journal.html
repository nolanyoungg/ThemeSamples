Field notes / 01

The value of a threshold

The moment of entering a building is rarely just a doorway. A change in ground, a sheltered edge, a framed view, or a shift in sound can all prepare us for the space beyond.

We think of a threshold as a small sequence. It should help people orient themselves without demanding attention. In public buildings, this clarity is part of feeling welcome.

Material study / 02

Timber, touched by light

A timber surface changes through the day. Direct light reveals its grain; softer light brings its colour into the room. These ordinary changes can give a simple interior a surprising depth.

The question is not only where timber appears, but how it meets other materials and where people encounter it. A handrail, a seat edge, or a reading surface can carry the character of the whole building.

Process / 03

Drawing as a conversation

A drawing is most useful when it helps someone see a decision. A plan can explain a route; a section can reveal the quality of light; a detail can make a material relationship clear.

We use different forms of drawing as a project develops. The aim is not to make every image complete, but to make each conversation more precise.
